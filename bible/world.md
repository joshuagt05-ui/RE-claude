# World Bible: How the World Ends in DOWNSTREAM

> *The missile was on time. The river was faster.*

This file holds the rules of the world. If a chapter breaks one of these rules, either the chapter is wrong or this file needs updating. Nothing here should contradict itself.

---

## 1. Where we break from canon

In the games and films, the government destroys Raccoon City and the outbreak mostly dies with it. **In DOWNSTREAM the strike happens on schedule, at 05:52 on the morning after the movie. It doesn't matter.**

Four days earlier, contaminated wastewater from Umbrella's lab under Raccoon City General started draining into the **Arklay River**. The Arklay runs out of the Arklay Mountains and into the **South Platte**. The South Platte feeds the **Platte**, the Platte feeds the **Missouri**, and the Missouri feeds the **Mississippi**. By the time the missile hits, the river is already three counties ahead of it.

## 2. What we keep from the movie

The night of the film is **Day 0**.

- Bryan, a medical courier whose girlfriend is pregnant, fights through Raccoon City General to the Umbrella lab on the 28th floor. He delivers the missing component of a T-virus antidote.
- The antidote is completed. Bryan is already turning. He kills the scientists, and **the only vial of antidote falls off the building to the street.**
- **Michelle**, Bryan's girlfriend, is pregnant and at home in another town. She has an unheard voice memo from him saying he's ready to be a father.
- **Maxine**, the Umbrella mercenary, is dead. The thing Bryan became is still alive when the screen goes black.

*(These film details come from reviews of the 2026 movie. Correct anything that's wrong and I'll update the bible.)*

In the book, the movie is a legend happening just over the horizon. The Mercers never learn Bryan's name, but the reader does. The vial and the thing in the hospital come back later as threads (see `outline/book-plan.md`).

## 3. The virus

| | |
|---|---|
| **Umbrella's name** | T-virus, Arklay variant (**T-ARK**) |
| **CDC name** (Day 7 on) | Raccoon City Virus (**RCV**) |
| **What the news calls it** | "Raccoon Fever" |
| **What people call the infected** | **the Turned**. Kids say *biters*. In later months, *dead-eyes*. |
| **Euphemism for the water-infected** | **"They drank."** A person who drank and hasn't turned yet is a *drinker*. By winter "he drank" is how you say someone is going to die. |

Each country and region should grow its own slang over the year. The Dispatches are where we show it.

### 3.1 The only two ways to catch it

**1. A bite from anything infected.**
- Any infected **mammal** counts: people, dogs, rats, cattle, horses, pigs, deer, elk and, later in the year, bats.
- Saliva has to get into the blood. A scratch doesn't do it. A bite does.
- **Incubation: 30 minutes to 6 hours.** Bites near the head or neck are fastest. A small bite on a hand or foot (a rat, for example) can take up to 12 hours.
- Infection is close to 100%. Cutting off the limb within a few minutes sometimes works. By winter, everyone carries a tourniquet.

**2. Drinking contaminated water.**
- Drinking gives a low dose, which means a **long, silent incubation of 4–10 days** (typically around 6).
- There are **no symptoms** until the last 12–24 hours. Then comes **the Thirst**: fever, a thirst that can't be satisfied, and confusion. The person collapses, then turns.
- **A drinker isn't contagious until they turn.** They can hug their kids, go to work and board a plane. **This is how it goes global.** Bites spread it within a town. Water spreads it along a river. Drinkers spread it around the world.
- Testing: from Month 2, a blood test with a 72-hour turnaround exists. From Month 5 there's a 20-minute field strip. By summer, test strips are a currency and checkpoints are organized around them.

### 3.2 What does *not* spread it
- Air, breath and coughing
- Touching or hugging a drinker
- Food, cooked or not *(open question: do we ever want a raw-meat scare? The rules say no.)*
- Mosquitoes, ticks and other insects
- Birds, reptiles and fish. **Birds are immune**, so the skies stay clean. Crows are still a problem, because they drop bits of carcasses into stock ponds and cisterns.

People **don't know** most of this for the first month. The misinformation is part of the world-building: people burn their pets, avoid rain, and believe you can catch it from money.

## 4. Water

This is the engine of the book. **Survival is a map of clean and dirty water.**

### 4.1 How water gets contaminated
- Blood or tissue from an infected body in the water, human or animal. At first this is Umbrella's leak. After that, it's everything that dies in a river.
- **"They go to the water."** The Thirst doesn't stop when someone turns, so **the Turned drift toward water.** They crowd riverbanks, fall into reservoirs and rot in stock ponds. Every infected person eventually poisons some water. This feedback loop is why the outbreak doesn't burn itself out, and it becomes the book's central image. The CDC figures it out around Day 20. The Mercers see it at their own stock pond first.

### 4.2 How long the virus survives
| Where | How long |
|---|---|
| Cold fresh water (under 50°F) | up to 6 weeks |
| Warm fresh water | 5–10 days |
| **Ice** | **Indefinitely.** Frozen ponds hold the virus until spring. |
| Salt water (over 2% salinity) | **Minutes.** The oceans are safe. |
| Brackish marshes and estuaries | Days. **Not safe.** |

### 4.3 What makes water safe
| Method | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rolling boil** | ✅ | 1 minute. **3 minutes above 6,500 ft**, which matters in Colorado. |
| Standard city chlorination | ❌ | The virus clumps in organic particles, and normal chlorine doses miss it. **This is why cities fall.** |
| Shock chlorination with filtering | ⚠️ | Works, but it takes modern plants, chemicals and power. Some cities hold out for a while this way. |
| UV sterilizer | ✅ | Only in clear water, and only while there's power. |
| **Reverse osmosis / desalination** | ✅ | Membranes stop it. RO units become worth more than guns. |
| Distillation | ✅ | Slow, and needs fuel. |
| Brita, ceramic or straw filters | ❌ | People believe they work for about three weeks. |
| **Rain**, collected clean | ✅ | Rain barrels become the most important object in the world. |
| **Fresh snow** | ✅ | Old snow near a carcass is not safe. |
| **Deep aquifer wells** | ✅ | Slow filtering through soil over time kills it. The Ogallala Aquifer is clean. |
| **Shallow wells near rivers** | ❌ | These draw river water through the gravel. **Merritt's irrigation wells are shallow.** |
| Sealed bottled water from before Day −4 | ✅ | **Except Arklay Springs** (§6). |

### 4.4 What this does to the world
- **Fuel becomes water.** Boiling takes fire. Wood, propane and natural gas become lifelines. Forests get stripped, and an electric kettle is useless without power.
- **Rivers become borders:** the Platte Line, the Missouri Line, the Mississippi Line, the Rhine.
- **Being upstream becomes power.** Whoever holds the headwaters and dams holds the people below them. Switzerland, the Himalayas, the Upper Nile and the Rockies all become flashpoints.
- **Coasts and desalination win.** Places with seawater desalination hold out: the Gulf states, Israel, Singapore, Perth, Southern California. The ocean is safe water once you strip out the salt.
- **Islands win at first.** Iceland, New Zealand, Japan, Tasmania and Hawai'i close their doors. Whether they can keep them closed is a story in itself.
- **Hydrology maps become survival maps.** The chapter maps show rivers in red and blue for this reason.

### 4.5 The seasons
- **Autumn (Onset):** warm water and fast spread.
- **Winter (the Quiet):** the Turned slow down below about 20°F and freeze solid below about 0°F. Fields are full of frozen bodies. People mistake the stillness for the end. **Frozen water holds the virus.**
- **Spring (the Thaw), the worst season:** frozen Turned wake up, ice releases a winter's worth of virus, and snowmelt floods spread it across floodplains. Every clean pond gets tested again.
- **Summer (the Rot):** heat speeds up decay. The first real hope appears, because the Turned are dying faster than new ones are made in some places. It's also drought season, when desperate people drink bad water.

## 5. The Turned

- **Human Turned:** classic Resident Evil zombies. They shuffle, then lunge at close range. They're drawn by sound, they never stop, and they drift toward water. They're killed by destroying the brain or by fire.
- **How long they last:** about **4–8 months** before their bodies give out. That means **the numbers start falling in Year One's summer.** The world's hope comes from this, not from a cure.
- **Animals:**
  - **Dogs:** fast, in packs, the Cerberus of the games. The most feared animal in Part I.
  - **Rats:** swarms. Small bites mean slower incubation.
  - **Cattle and horses:** huge, and they stampede. On the plains, **an infected herd is a natural disaster.**
  - **Deer and elk:** spread it through the Rockies with nobody watching.
  - **Bats (summer):** this is when it escalates, because bats are flying mammals that bite.
- **Mutations:** mutations are rare and escalate slowly. Heavy doses or re-infection produce advanced forms. The first reports come after the Thaw (March). By Part IV, "Lickers" is a word people use. We **ration these carefully.** Each one should be an event, not a monster of the week. The biggest mutation in the world is the thing Bryan became, and it's still in the ruins of Raccoon City.

## 6. Umbrella

- **Where it started:** the Arklay lab under Raccoon City General.
- **The bottled water:** Umbrella's premium "Rocky Mountain" brand, **Arklay Springs**, draws from the Arklay Reservoir downstream of the lab. **Lot 0922**, bottled on Day −3, holds **1.4 million bottles shipped to 31 countries.** Umbrella recalls it quietly on Day 4, calling it "mineral imbalance." About 70% has already reached shelves. **This is the second way the virus goes global, and it's Umbrella's crime.**
- **The cover-up:** the official story of the Day 1 strike is "an explosion at an Umbrella chemical facility." Umbrella's stock is halted on Day 2. Its executives disappear into private retreats.
- **The Arklay Files (around Day 60):** an Umbrella whistleblower leaks the internal record: the leak, the recall and the fact that the antidote existed. The world learns there was a cure for one night.
- **The trial (spring):** the Hague tries Umbrella in absentia.
- **The hope thread:** did the antidote formula die with the scientists on the 28th floor, or is it sitting in an Umbrella data vault? This is left open for the final Dispatch.

## 7. How the world breaks: seven failing systems

In the family chapters, one system fails at a time, so each part of the book gets its own kind of fear.

1. **Truth.** The news and the government lie, then contradict each other, then go silent. (Part I)
2. **Medicine.** Hospitals fill up, supplies run out, and **insulin runs out.** (Parts I–III)
3. **Law.** Cordons, martial law, sheriffs who stay or quit, militias. (Part II)
4. **Supply.** Trucks stop, and shelves empty in a week. (Part II)
5. **Power.** Rolling blackouts, then the grid goes dark. Walt predicts it to the week. (Part III)
6. **Communication.** Cell service, then the internet, then everything except ham radio. (Part III)
7. **Government.** The federal government breaks apart, and the Pacific Authority rises. (Parts III–V)

## 8. Glimpses of the wider world

The main story stays with the family. The rest of the world reaches the reader through **five windows**:

1. **Grandpa Walt's ham radio.** His shack has a world map on the wall, with a pin for every station he's ever contacted. Over the year the pins go quiet one by one, and new ones appear in strange places (a ship, Antarctica, a Swiss bunker). Each contact is a short scene with a real voice.
2. **Aunt Carmen.** She flies DEN→Frankfurt on Day 3 and gets stranded. She's the family's personal window into Europe through calls, then texts, then Walt's radio relay. Her trip north ends in Iceland.
3. **Screens, while they last.** TV, Nora's phone, livestreams and leaked videos. These get **worse and less frequent through Part II** and are gone by Part III. The loss is part of the story.
4. **Travelers.** Refugees, soldiers and truckers bring rumors, and some of those rumors turn out to be wrong.
5. **The Dispatches.** Twelve short standalone chapters, one per month, each set somewhere else in the world with a different point-of-view character and its own map. They're how we go global without leaving the family. The full list is in `outline/book-plan.md`.

## 9. The world at the one-year mark: "the Blue Map"

The last map of the book shows the world at Day 365. It's mostly red, but not entirely:
- **Blue zones:** coasts with desalination, islands that held, high-mountain headwaters, and regions over deep aquifers.
- The Turned are in slow decline. Mutations are rarer but worse.
- New powers: the Pacific Authority, the Alpine Redoubt, the desalination states, Japan.
- No cure. Maybe a rumor of one.

The world has ended, and a new one is starting. The final line of the book belongs to the Mercers.
