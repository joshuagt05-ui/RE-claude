# Characters

These are drafts. Change names, ages or anything else you like. Anything marked **(open)** is still an open decision.

---

## The Mercers of Merritt, Colorado

A fourth-generation farm family with 900 acres along the South Platte: sugar beets, corn and a couple hundred head of cattle. The farmhouse is a century old. Walt lives in the old bunkhouse. The family has one good truck, one bad truck and one dog.

### Nora Mercer (17) · *primary POV*
- High school senior and cross-country runner. She has already written her CU Boulder application essay, about wanting to leave Merritt.
- She's smart, restless and sometimes cruel in the way seventeen-year-olds are. She's also braver than she thinks, and sees herself clearly.
- She keeps a **voice journal** on her phone. When the phone dies, she switches to a paper notebook. Some chapters could end on journal entries.
- **Arc:** she spends the whole year wanting to get out, and ends it as the person who gets her family out. Boulder is ash. She never gets to leave home, because home leaves her first.

### June Mercer, née Ybarra (45)
- ER charge nurse at the Logan County hospital in Sterling, 25 minutes up I-76.
- **She sees it first.** Patient One arrives on her shift on Day 6. She knows how bad it is before the TV does, and she has to decide what to tell her kids.
- Calm in a crisis, and terrible at being calm at home. She's the family's medic, which puts Eli's insulin on her shoulders.
- **Arc:** triage. Who can she save? She keeps choosing the hospital over her family until the day she can't.

### Ray Mercer (47)
- The farmer. He talks little and his humor is dry. He's gentle with animals and stubborn about land.
- He knows the river: every ditch, headgate and stock pond. That makes him the first to understand **what the water means**, and the last to accept it.
- **Arc:** from keeping the farm to letting it go. Leaving the farm (Day ~178) is his chapter.

### Eli Mercer (10)
- **Type 1 diabetic.** His insulin has to stay refrigerated, and there's only so much of it. **That's a clock ticking through the whole book.**
- **He's obsessed with maps.** His bedroom wall is National Geographic maps, and he draws his own. The in-world reason every chapter has a map is Eli's atlas.
- He's funny, anxious and precise. He notices things, and he's the first to notice that "they go to the water."
- **Arc:** he reads the world through maps and learns that the map isn't the territory. His last map is the ocean.

### Walt Ybarra (74)
- June's father, a widower, living in the bunkhouse.
- **Retired lineman** for the rural electric co-op. He knows exactly how the grid will fail and roughly when ("eight weeks, maybe ten").
- **Ham radio operator** for fifty years. His shack wall has a world map with a pin for every station he's ever contacted. **He's the family's window on the world**, and he hears it go quiet one voice at a time.
- **Arc:** the old world's knowledge in a new world. **(open)** Does Walt survive the year? His death, if it happens, would be the heart of Part III.

### Carmen Ybarra (38)
- June's younger sister and the fun aunt. International flight attendant based at DEN.
- **Works DEN→Frankfurt on Day 3.** She's stranded in Europe as the Rhine falls, heads north, and makes the last flight Iceland accepts.
- **She's the family's thread abroad.** She reaches them through phone calls, then texts, then Walt's radio relay. She also narrates Dispatch X from Reykjavík.
- **Arc:** she spends the year trying to get home and then, maybe, chooses not to.

### Biscuit (6)
- Australian shepherd who herds everything, including Eli.
- **(open)** In a world where anything infected that bites can kill you, the family dog carries real stakes. Readers will need to know: does Biscuit make it? My vote: **yes.** The book should hold back one mercy.
